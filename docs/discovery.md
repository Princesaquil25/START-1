# Career Exploration App Discovery

This document records the early product decisions and research for the
career exploration and navigation app. No application code is being built in
this phase.

## Day 1: Target users and problem

### Target users

Adults changing careers, regardless of their industry or education background.

### Problem statement

Adults changing careers struggle to connect their existing skills and
experience to a manageable shortlist of realistic career paths.

### Intended outcome

The app should help a career changer leave with a short, understandable list
of career paths that appear to fit their transferable skills and past
experience.

Suggestions should be presented as possibilities to explore, not guarantees.

## Day 2: Existing tools and resources

### CareerOneStop Skills Matcher

- **What it does:** Matches self-identified workplace skills with occupations
  that use similar skills.
- **Information requested:** The user rates their level on workplace skills,
  such as analyzing information, communicating, or managing people.
- **Results:** Suggested occupations with information about tasks, education,
  wages, and job outlook.
- **Useful for career changers:** It focuses directly on transferable skills.
- **Limitations:** It relies on accurate self-assessment and may not capture
  the person's interests, values, circumstances, or the context of their
  previous experience.
- **Source:** [CareerOneStop Skills Matcher](https://www.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Skills/skills-matcher.aspx)

### My Next Move

- **What it does:** Helps users browse and explore hundreds of occupations.
- **Information requested:** Keywords, an industry selection, or answers to
  an interest assessment.
- **Results:** Career pages covering tasks, skills, work activities,
  education, wages, and job outlook.
- **Useful for career changers:** It provides accessible background
  information when a person is unfamiliar with a possible career.
- **Limitations:** It is mainly an exploration and information resource. It
  does not deeply connect a person's work history to a personalized transition
  plan.
- **Source:** [My Next Move](https://www.mynextmove.org/)

### O*NET Interest Profiler

- **What it does:** Helps users understand what kinds of work activities they
  may enjoy.
- **Information requested:** Preferences about activities such as designing,
  helping people, analyzing information, or working with tools.
- **Results:** An interest profile and occupations related to those interests.
- **Useful for career changers:** It can help someone who knows their current
  work is not satisfying but has not identified what they want next.
- **Limitations:** Interests do not measure skills, qualifications, income
  needs, or job availability. An interesting result may not be immediately
  practical.
- **Source:** [O*NET Interest Profiler](https://www.onetcenter.org/IP.html)

### LinkedIn career resources

- **What it does:** Uses professional profile information, skills, learning
  content, and job data to support career exploration.
- **Information requested:** Depending on the feature, job titles, work
  history, listed skills, education, and desired roles.
- **Results:** Possible related roles, overlapping skills, skill gaps, jobs,
  courses, and professional connections.
- **Useful for career changers:** It connects exploration to real jobs,
  learning resources, and people who may have made similar transitions.
- **Limitations:** It works best with a complete, current profile. Results
  may favor careers and skills that are well represented in LinkedIn's
  professional network. Features may vary by account and location.
- **Source:** [LinkedIn career resources](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507663)

## Ideas to consider for a beginner project

The first version does not need to replace these tools. A manageable
beginner-sized concept is:

> Ask an adult career changer about past experience and transferable skills,
> then present three to five possible career paths with a short explanation of
> why each path may be worth exploring.

Potential first-version ideas:

1. Ask for previous roles, responsibilities, skills, and activities the user
   enjoys.
2. Translate the user's plain-language experience into a simple transferable
   skills list.
3. Present three to five possible career directions instead of an overwhelming
   list.
4. Explain which user-provided skills or experiences relate to each suggestion.
5. Let users add or remove information and indicate when a suggestion does not
   fit.
6. Link to trusted resources such as O*NET or CareerOneStop for occupation
   details.
7. Clearly label results as possibilities to explore rather than predictions,
   guarantees, or promises of employment.
